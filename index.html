About

I am a researcher working at the intersection of artificial intelligence, fraud detection, and financial crime. My work focuses on building explainable and responsible AI systems that can be deployed in high-stakes domains including banking, insurance, and digital payments.

I completed my PhD at IIT Delhi, where I developed graph-based and deep learning approaches for anomaly and fraud detection. I am currently a Visiting Scholar in the Fraud Research Group at Aston University, supported by UKRI funding, working on AI-driven solutions to financial crime with academic and industry partners.

My broader interests span responsible AI, synthetic data generation, explainability (XAI), and the application of machine learning to complex real-world problems. I am passionate about bridging rigorous research with meaningful societal impact.

Research

My research focuses on three interconnected themes: fraud detection, explainable AI, and responsible deployment of machine learning in financial and regulatory contexts.

Fraud Detection & Financial Crime

Graph neural networks, anomaly detection, and behavioural analytics applied to payment fraud, identity theft, and money laundering detection at scale.

Explainable AI (XAI)

Developing interpretable models and post-hoc explanation methods that make AI decisions transparent and auditable for regulators, businesses, and end users.

Graph & Deep Learning

Graph-based representation learning for relational fraud patterns, including heterogeneous graphs, temporal dynamics, and semi-supervised detection methods.

Synthetic Data & Privacy

Generative approaches for synthetic financial data to enable model training while preserving privacy and satisfying regulatory constraints.
